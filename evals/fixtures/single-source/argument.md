# Synthetic Argument: Meaningful Consent

Claim: a policy should give people a way to withdraw consent for future use of their contributions.

Premise 1: a person's preferences can change after consent is given.
Premise 2: meaningful consent requires an opportunity to act on such changes.
Intermediate step: an irrevocable permission for all future uses would remove that opportunity.

The author concludes that future use should have a withdrawal mechanism. The argument does not establish that every past use can be undone. Other people may already have relied on past permissions, and some consequences may be irreversible. The author acknowledges this objection but does not resolve the boundary between protecting reliance and permitting withdrawal.

The term meaningful consent is used in the normative sense of permission that remains responsive to a person's agency, not a claim about any jurisdiction's law.
